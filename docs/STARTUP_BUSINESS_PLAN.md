# ZENDOC — Startup Business Plan & Capital Roadmap

**Planning date:** September 2026  
**Purpose:** Founder operating plan and investor-use-of-funds framework.  
**Important:** Budget figures are planning ranges, not supplier quotations. Regulated activities must be launched only after the applicable licence, registration, clinical/legal review, or authorised partner integration.

## One-line business plan

**ZENDOC is a freemium AI healthcare operating system that connects patients and families to verified care, longitudinal health records, government/insurance benefits, medicines, diagnostics, devices and payments, while monetising provider SaaS, enterprise infrastructure and lawful transactions—starting asset-light and adding owned fulfilment only after local demand density is proven.**

## 1. Strategic rule

ZENDOC must not begin by owning every physical asset.

The correct sequence is:

1. Own the patient experience, trust layer and healthcare orchestration software.
2. Aggregate verified doctors, hospitals, labs, pharmacies, schemes and benefits.
3. Generate demand and measure actual transaction density.
4. Monetise software and partner transactions.
5. Add micro-fulfilment inventory only in locations where it improves service and unit economics.
6. Add owned logistics only when route density beats third-party delivery economics.
7. Expand district-by-district and state-by-state.

This avoids wasting investor capital on empty warehouses, idle riders and expiring medicine inventory.

## 7. Physical healthcare layer — only after proof

### A. Micro-fulfilment medicine warehouse

#### When needed
Only after the platform has recurring local orders and partner-pharmacy fulfilment creates excessive cancellations, delays or margin leakage.

#### Regulatory premise
Drug storage/sale must use the appropriate drug licence and compliant premises/personnel. Do not operate medicine inventory from an ordinary office.

#### Planning footprint
Start with **1,000–2,000 sq ft**, not a giant warehouse.

#### Setup budget excluding inventory
| Item | Planning range |
|---|---:|
| Deposit + initial rent | ₹1L–₹3L |
| Racking/bins/work tables | ₹1L–₹2L |
| Refrigeration/cold-chain equipment | ₹1L–₹3L |
| Temperature monitoring/data loggers | ₹25k–₹75k |
| Power backup | ₹50k–₹1.5L |
| CCTV/access/fire safety | ₹50k–₹1L |
| Barcode/scanners/printers | ₹30k–₹75k |
| licences/professional/compliance setup | ₹50k–₹1.5L |
| warehouse software/integration | ₹25k–₹75k |

**Indicative setup: ₹5L–₹12L before inventory.**

#### Medicine inventory working capital
Small launch:
**₹8L–₹25L**

Inventory must be based on actual demand, expiry risk, cold-chain requirements, prescription rules and supplier credit—not a broad speculative catalogue.

### B. Medical/IoT devices

Start as marketplace + rental before owning large stocks.

Starter owned inventory after demand proof:
**₹5L–₹15L**

Potential categories:
- BP monitors
- glucometers
- pulse oximeters
- thermometers
- weighing/smart scales

### C. Delivery fleet

#### Phase 1
Use third-party delivery partners.

#### Phase 2
Hybrid model:
- third-party normal delivery

### D. Staff after warehouse launch

Indicative monthly planning:

## 8. Warehouse trigger — investors should see discipline

Open a warehouse only if at least several of these conditions hold for 2–3 consecutive months:

- 100+ medicine/device orders/day in the service cluster
- repeat-order rate >30%
- partner fill rate is materially below target
- order cancellation from unavailable stock >5–8%
- gross contribution lost to third-party fulfilment exceeds projected warehouse fixed cost
- top 300–500 SKUs account for the majority of demand
- expiry-adjusted inventory turns are attractive
- route density supports same-day fulfilment
- licences and pharmacist/quality staff are ready

If these gates are not met, continue asset-light.

## 11. Unit economics investors should demand

For every transaction track:

**Revenue**
- subscription revenue
- provider SaaS allocation
- contracted service/transaction fee
- delivery income
- device rental/sale margin

**Variable cost**
- payment fee
- delivery
- support
- API/model cost
- packaging
- returns
- inventory spoilage/expiry
- partner payout

**Contribution margin = Revenue - variable cost**

ZENDOC should not scale a physical channel until contribution margin is positive or has a credible near-term path to positive.

Core metrics:
- CAC
- activation
- MAU/WAU
- 30/90-day retention
- provider retention
- GMV
- ARR/MRR
- revenue per user/provider
- contribution margin
- order fill rate
- delivery cost/order
- inventory turns
- expiry/write-off %
- CareFin savings discovered
- CareFin savings actually confirmed
- time-to-appointment
- patient out-of-pocket reduction
- clinical/safety incidents

## 12. Investor narrative

### Problem
Indian healthcare is fragmented across discovery, records, providers, pharmacies, diagnostics, insurance, government benefits and payments. Patients repeatedly provide the same information and often pay more than necessary because the systems do not coordinate.

### Solution
ZENDOC provides a consent-aware healthcare operating layer that connects these systems and uses bounded agentic AI to plan and coordinate safe actions.

### Wedge
Begin with:
**health memory + provider discovery + family care + CareFin + local medicine/diagnostic fulfilment.**

### Moat
- longitudinal authorised patient context
- provider network
- benefits/coverage graph
- geographic healthcare graph
- agentic orchestration and permissioned actions
- operational fulfilment data
- trust/provenance/audit layer
- integration depth
- switching value from accumulated workflows, not user lock-in

### Expansion
Nadia → Kolkata → West Bengal → India → international healthcare corridors.

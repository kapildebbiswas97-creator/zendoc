# ZENDOC Investor Readiness

## Purpose

This document defines what ZENDOC should be able to prove before actively fundraising.

A competition result, award, or accelerator selection is useful social proof, but it is not required for fundraising and must not substitute for product evidence.

## Fundraising Stages

### Pre-seed readiness
Recommended evidence:
- working production-pilot product;
- founder commitment and clear ownership;
- problem interviews;
- first real users;
- repeat usage or strong pilot demand;
- clear target market;
- documented business model;
- defensible architecture/data strategy;
- product roadmap;
- basic security/privacy posture;
- credible use of funds.

### Seed readiness
Stronger evidence:
- repeatable user acquisition;
- retention;
- provider/institution participation;
- measurable care-journey completion;
- revenue or strong contracted pilots;
- scalable onboarding;
- compliance/legal work appropriate to operating markets;
- production observability and incident response;
- clear unit economics.

Track at least:

- WAU / MAU;
- D7 and D30 retention;
- searches per active user;
- useful-result rate;
- completed appointments/care actions;
- geography nodes with official provenance;
- provider/facility records by source;
- stale-data rate;

- pilot pipeline;

- support incidents;
- safety incidents.

## Fundraising Narrative Discipline

Never say:
- nationwide coverage is complete if official rows are missing;
- all hospitals are connected if records are only public-directory entries;
- pharmacy stock is live without inventory integration;
- ambulance dispatch is live without dispatch confirmation;
- regulatory compliance is complete without appropriate legal/technical validation;
- AI is clinically validated without evidence.

Prefer:
- "architecture supports all India; imported coverage is measured by source and geography";
- "official directory record";
- "integration required";
- "live availability unavailable";
- "production pilot";
- "clinical validation planned / under review" where accurate.

## Likely Investor Questions

### Why now?
Answer with healthcare digitization, public digital infrastructure, increasing consumer expectations, fragmented provider workflows, and increasing AI capability — but support any quantitative claim with current sources.

### Why ZENDOC?
Show product workflow, trust model, longitudinal context, geographic data layer, provider/institution platform, and orchestration.

### What is the wedge?
The initial wedge should be a high-frequency, painful healthcare workflow that can be measured. Candidate wedges include trustworthy local healthcare discovery + continuity, family care coordination, or provider-connected follow-up.

### How do you acquire users?
Demonstrate specific channels rather than saying "social media":
- college/community pilots;
- provider referrals;
- pharmacy/lab partnerships;
- employer/NGO pilots;
- local-language content;
- search/discovery utility;
- patient referral loops.

### Why can't a large company copy this?
The answer cannot be "AI." Defensibility should compound through:
- integrations;
- trusted source normalization;
- longitudinal authorized context;
- operational workflows;
- provider network;
- user trust;
- data quality;
- local execution;
- regulatory/process knowledge.

## Investor Outreach Process

1. build a target list by stage and thesis;
2. prioritize healthcare, SaaS, digital public infrastructure, AI, and India-market investors;
3. seek warm introductions where possible;
4. send a concise founder note with problem, traction, wedge, and ask;
5. track all outreach;
6. run fundraising as a pipeline;
7. update interested investors with real progress;
8. avoid mass-spamming generic decks.

## Before Asking for Money

ZENDOC should ideally demonstrate:
- a reliable live product;
- a small but real user cohort;
- at least one repeatable use case;
- evidence that users return;
- evidence that providers/institutions care;
- a clear next 12-month plan;
- a specific funding amount tied to milestones.

## Use of Funds Framework

Early capital should primarily fund:
- engineering and infrastructure;
- data quality and integrations;
- security/privacy/compliance;
- provider onboarding and operations;
- clinical governance;
- focused go-to-market;
- key hires.

Avoid spending early capital on broad branding, large teams, or nationwide field operations before product-market evidence.

## Decision Rule

Do not wait for "perfect" before talking to investors.

Start relationship-building early, but raise aggressively when ZENDOC can show evidence that capital will accelerate an already-working loop.
